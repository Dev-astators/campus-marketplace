# Meetings Stand-up Summary

## Overview

Throughout the project, the team used stand-up meetings to discuss progress, identify blockers, assign tasks, and prepare for sprint deadlines. These meetings were important for keeping the team aligned, especially as the project became more complex and the workload from other modules increased.

Although communication was not always consistent, the team continued to make progress across the sprints. The quality of communication improved over time, and the stand-ups helped the team identify technical problems, adjust priorities, and complete most of the required functionality before final submission.

## Sprint 1 Summary

During Sprint 1, the team experienced communication challenges. Updates were not always shared clearly or frequently enough, which made it difficult at times to track individual progress and task ownership. Some team members worked independently without giving enough regular feedback to the rest of the group.

Despite these communication issues, the team still managed to complete the required work for the sprint. This was mainly due to individual responsibility, follow-ups near the deadline, and the willingness of team members to complete assigned tasks even when coordination was not ideal.

### What Went Wrong

- Communication was poor during the early stages of the sprint.
- Task ownership was not always clearly confirmed after meetings.
- Progress updates were sometimes delayed.
- Some blockers were only raised close to the deadline.
- The team had not yet developed a consistent meeting structure.

### What Went Well

- The required sprint work was still completed.
- Team members showed responsibility for their assigned tasks.
- The sprint helped the team understand the importance of clearer communication.
- The team gained a better understanding of the project scope and expectations.

### Areas for Improvement

- Hold more structured stand-up meetings with clear agendas.
- Confirm task ownership at the end of each meeting.
- Share progress updates more frequently.
- Raise blockers earlier so they can be resolved before deadlines.
- Use the project board or task-tracking tools more consistently.

## Sprint 2 Summary

Sprint 2 showed improvement compared to Sprint 1. The team had a better understanding of the system requirements and began coordinating tasks more effectively. Stand-up discussions helped the team divide work and follow up on the progress of key features.

However, communication was still not fully consistent. Some updates were still informal, and certain implementation details could have been documented more clearly. The team also learned that testing should happen earlier in the sprint rather than being left close to the deadline.

### What Went Wrong

- Some communication still happened too late or outside formal meetings.
- Testing and review were not always done early enough.
- Documentation was not always updated at the same time as development.
- Some decisions were discussed verbally but not written down clearly.

### What Went Well

- Task allocation improved compared to Sprint 1.
- The team became more familiar with the application structure.
- Core functionality continued to progress.
- Members became more comfortable asking for help and giving updates.

### Areas for Improvement

- Document meeting decisions immediately.
- Test completed features earlier.
- Keep sprint documentation updated throughout the sprint.
- Maintain clearer communication between frontend, backend, and deployment tasks.

## Sprint 3 Summary

During Sprint 3, communication was moderate. The team held meetings and continued to work toward the sprint goals, but availability was affected by other school modules, assessments, and a high academic workload. This made it more difficult to maintain consistent progress and attend every meeting with the same level of focus.

A major issue in Sprint 3 was that the deployed site went down. The problem was caused by a mismatch between the environment variables used on localhost and the environment variables configured on Azure. Because the local development environment worked differently from the deployed environment, the issue was only fully noticed during deployment.

This created a setback, but it also helped the team understand the importance of deployment configuration, environment consistency, and testing the production environment before submission deadlines.

### What Went Wrong

- The deployed site was down due to environment variable mismatch between localhost and Azure.
- Deployment settings were not checked early enough.
- The team underestimated the risk of differences between local and production environments.
- Other academic work affected meeting attendance and development time.
- Some issues were discovered close to the sprint deadline.

### What Went Well

- The team continued holding meetings despite workload pressure.
- The deployment issue was identified and investigated.
- Members continued completing assigned tasks where possible.
- The team gained important experience with deployment and production configuration.

### Areas for Improvement

- Create a shared environment variable checklist for local and deployed environments.
- Test the deployed site earlier in the sprint.
- Assign responsibility for checking deployment health after major changes.
- Keep deployment documentation up to date.
- Communicate availability issues early so tasks can be reassigned if necessary.

## Sprint 4 Summary

In Sprint 4, communication improved compared to the earlier sprints. The team held meetings, followed up on outstanding work, and focused on preparing the project for final submission. The deployment issues from Sprint 3 were fixed, and the site was stabilized for the final sprint and final submission.

However, the team was still affected by heavy workload from other modules. Because of time pressure, not every planned feature was completed. In particular, the review flagging functionality was not completed for Sprint 4. Even though this was a limitation, the team was able to complete most of the required functionality and resolve the most important deployment problems.

### What Went Wrong

- The review flagging functionality was not completed in time for Sprint 4.
- Time pressure from other modules reduced the team's availability.
- Some work was completed later than planned.
- The team still needed better prioritisation of high-risk features.

### What Went Well

- The Azure deployment issue from Sprint 3 was resolved.
- The final deployed site was stabilized.
- Most required functionality was completed.
- Communication improved through meetings and follow-ups.
- The team focused on preparing the project for final submission.

### Areas for Improvement

- Prioritise incomplete or high-risk features earlier in the sprint.
- Leave more time for final testing and bug fixing.
- Reassign tasks earlier when members are unavailable.
- Track unfinished features more visibly during stand-ups.
- Separate development time from documentation and testing time more clearly.

## Overall Reflection

Overall, the stand-up meetings helped the team stay aligned and continue making progress, even when communication and availability were not perfect. The biggest communication challenge happened in Sprint 1, where updates were not shared consistently and responsibilities were not always clear. Over time, the team improved by holding more meetings, following up on tasks, and becoming more aware of blockers.

The main technical challenge was the deployment issue in Sprint 3, where the site went down because of environment variable differences between localhost and Azure. This problem was fixed in Sprint 4 and for the final submission. The experience showed the team that deployment configuration must be treated as a key part of development, not something to check only at the end.

By the final submission, the team had completed most of the required functionality and resolved the major deployment problems. The only significant missing functionality was the review flagging feature planned for Sprint 4. Even with this limitation, the team showed improvement in communication, technical problem-solving, and coordination across the project.

## Key Lessons Learned

- Clear communication is essential from the first sprint.
- Stand-up meetings are more effective when each member gives a short update on completed work, current tasks, and blockers.
- Task ownership should always be confirmed after meetings.
- Deployment should be tested throughout the sprint, not only near the deadline.
- Environment variables must be consistent between local development and production.
- Academic workload should be considered when planning sprint capacity.
- High-risk features should be started earlier to avoid last-minute pressure.
- Documentation should be updated continuously instead of being left until final submission.

## Future Improvements

For future projects, the team would improve by using a more structured stand-up format. Each meeting should include what was completed, what is currently being worked on, what blockers exist, and what must be completed before the next meeting. This would make progress easier to track and reduce confusion around responsibilities.

The team would also improve by maintaining a shared deployment checklist, testing the deployed application earlier, and documenting important configuration details. Better planning around other academic responsibilities would help the team set more realistic sprint goals and avoid leaving important features until the final stages of the project.

Overall, the project helped the team improve not only technical skills, but also collaboration, communication, planning, and deployment awareness.
